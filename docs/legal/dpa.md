## 1. Definitions

- **"Data Protection Law"** means the EU General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 ("GDPR"), the UK GDPR and the UK Data Protection Act 2018, the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection ("FADP"), and any national law implementing or supplementing them, each as applicable to the processing under this DPA.
- **"Personal Data"**, **"Controller"**, **"Processor"**, **"Data Subject"**, **"Processing"**, **"Supervisory Authority"** and **"Personal Data Breach"** have the meanings given in the GDPR.
- **"Customer Personal Data"** means Personal Data that the Customer, its users, or its devices submit to the Service and that the Provider processes on the Customer's behalf. The categories are described in Annex I.
- **"Sub-processor"** means a third party engaged by the Provider to process Customer Personal Data.
- **"EU SCCs"** means the standard contractual clauses for the transfer of personal data to third countries adopted by the European Commission in Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 of 4 June 2021.
- **"UK Addendum"** means the International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Commission Standard Contractual Clauses issued by the UK Information Commissioner under section 119A of the Data Protection Act 2018, Version B1.0, in force from 21 March 2022.
- **"Restricted Transfer"** means a transfer of Customer Personal Data to a country outside the EEA, the UK, or Switzerland (as applicable) that is not covered by an adequacy decision.
- **"Technical and Organisational Measures"** or **"TOMs"** means the measures described in Annex II.

## 2. Roles and scope

2.1. For Customer Personal Data, the Customer is the Controller and the Provider is the Processor. Where the Customer itself acts as a processor for a third-party controller, the Customer warrants that its instructions to the Provider are authorised by that controller, and the Provider acts as a sub-processor.

2.2. This DPA does not apply to Personal Data for which the Provider is itself a Controller: the account identity of the individual who registers (email address, credentials), billing and tax records of the Customer entity, contact-form and feedback submissions addressed to the Provider, and error reports the dashboard sends to the Provider. That processing is governed by the PidgeIoT Privacy Policy.

2.3. Each party will comply with its own obligations under Data Protection Law. The Customer is responsible for the lawfulness of the Personal Data it and its devices send to the Service, including any notices to and legal basis for Data Subjects, and for configuring the Service (devices, telemetry keys, alert channels, forwarding endpoints) so that it does not send Personal Data it is not entitled to send.

## 3. Subject matter, duration, nature and purpose

3.1. **Subject matter.** The processing of Customer Personal Data that occurs when the Customer uses the Service to provision, authenticate, configure, monitor, update and alert on its IoT devices, and to manage the people in its organisation who do so.

3.2. **Duration.** For the term of the Agreement and until Customer Personal Data has been deleted or returned under Section 8.

3.3. **Nature.** Collection from the Customer's devices and dashboard users; storage; retrieval and display; transmission to endpoints the Customer configures; deletion. The processing is automated and hosted.

3.4. **Purpose.** Providing the Service as described in the Agreement and the published API documentation, and as further instructed by the Customer through its use of the dashboard and API. The Provider does not process Customer Personal Data for its own purposes, does not sell it, does not use it to train models for any purpose other than providing the Service to that Customer, and does not build profiles. This does not apply to an optional program the Customer affirmatively enrolls in under separate terms. The Provider may derive aggregated statistics about use of the Service, such as account, device and message counts, that do not identify, and cannot reasonably be used to identify, any customer, device or person, and may use them to operate, secure, improve and describe the Service; it does not use the contents of Customer Personal Data for that purpose.

3.5. The categories of Personal Data and of Data Subjects are set out in Annex I.

4.6. **Assistance with security, breach notification and DPIAs (Art. 28(3)(f), Arts. 32 to 36).** Assist the Customer in ensuring compliance with Articles 32 to 36 GDPR, taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to the Provider, including by providing the information in Annex II and the published sub-processor list for the Customer's data protection impact assessments and prior consultations.

4.7. **Deletion or return (Art. 28(3)(g)).** At the Customer's choice, delete or return Customer Personal Data at the end of the provision of the Service under Section 8.

4.8. **Demonstrating compliance and audits (Art. 28(3)(h)).** Make available to the Customer all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with Article 28 GDPR and allow for and contribute to audits under Section 7.

## Annex II: Technical and Organisational Measures

Completes Annex II of the EU SCCs. Written from the deployed architecture. Where a measure depends on a Sub-processor's own controls, Section H says which one and what the Provider relies on.

### A. Access control to systems and data

1. **Dashboard authentication** is a self-hosted Ory Kratos identity server. Password login with server-side hashing; passkeys (WebAuthn), TOTP authenticator apps and one-time lookup secrets are available as second factors. Sessions last four hours. The session cookie is HttpOnly and scoped to the service domain. Any request that no longer resolves to a valid session signs the browser out, so an expired or revoked session cannot render another user's or an empty account's data.
2. **Authorisation is enforced server-side on every request.** The edge router resolves the session to a user identifier and forwards only that identifier to the device store; nothing in a request body can assert an identity. Organisations have three roles (owner, admin, member) with a documented per-route permission matrix; every organisation must retain at least one owner. Each device carries its own access-control list inside its own storage object and refuses any user not on it. Cross-device queries in the relational mirror carry the owner or membership predicate in the query itself.
3. **Device authentication** does not use shared secrets over HTTPS. Each device receives its own Ed25519 keypair at provisioning; only the public key is stored, the private key signs one bearer token and is discarded. A token contains a version, an expiry and a signature, and nothing else, so it is bound to a device only by the public key that verifies it. Rotating a device's token generates a new keypair and revokes every earlier token immediately. Over CoAP and over MQTT, each device holds its own TLS-PSK identity and secret, minted at provisioning and served to the terminator or broker only from an allow-listed source address.
4. **Administrative access.** The identity server's administrative API binds to loopback on its host and is never published. The administrative console reaches it only on the same host, and is reachable from outside only through a Cloudflare Access application with a single-person allow-list and a 24-hour session.
5. **Host access.** SSH to the VPS is by public key only; password and keyboard-interactive authentication are disabled, root login is key-only, and repeated failed connections are banned automatically. The host firewall drops all inbound traffic except SSH and the device transport port; the identity server is reachable only through an outbound Cloudflare Tunnel. Configuration containing credentials is root-only with mode 0600.
6. **Secrets handling.** Worker secrets are set with the deployment tool's secret command and never appear in configuration files or the repository. Credential values are never printed, logged, committed or pasted into chat; they are referred to by name and read from the environment. Invite tokens are stored only as a hash. Forwarding-endpoint bearer tokens and device credentials are never returned by read APIs.

### B. Transmission control (encryption in transit)

1. Browser and API traffic terminates on the edge provider over TLS. Traffic from the edge to the managed PostgreSQL instance uses TLS (StartTLS through the connection pooler); the database requires TLS 1.2 or higher. Traffic from the edge to the identity server crosses a Cloudflare Tunnel.
2. Device traffic uses TLS over HTTPS or WebSocket, DTLS 1.2 with connection identifiers over UDP and TLS over TCP for CoAP, or TLS for MQTT, each authenticated per device as in A.3.
3. Internal calls between the edge router and each device's storage object stay inside the edge provider's network and are encrypted by that provider.

### C. Storage control (encryption at rest) and isolation

1. Per-device state (configuration, latest telemetry, logs, access list, device public key) is held in an isolated storage object per device; a request to one device's object cannot read another's. The edge provider encrypts that storage at rest (AES-256, provider-managed keys).
2. The relational mirror and the identity database are on a single-tenant managed PostgreSQL instance with AES-256 encryption at rest and a network isolated from other customers.
3. Firmware images are stored in a private object-storage bucket accessible only through the edge router, which checks the device's own credential before serving a byte range.
4. Production and staging share no data plane: separate storage namespaces, databases, buckets and queues.
5. Telemetry that the Customer forwards to its own endpoint is sent there and not retained as history by the Provider; the endpoint is the Customer's responsibility.

### D. Input control and minimisation

1. Every write is attributable to a session identity, a device credential, or a service principal. Organisation membership records who invited whom and when. Device storage bumps a version on each shadow write and stamps every telemetry key with its own report time.
2. Dashboard error reports are de-identified by design and carry no direct account identifier: no user identity, no full URLs, no query strings, no form contents, no request or response bodies. The server re-normalises messages and routes, replacing identifiers, email addresses and token-shaped strings with placeholders before storing the long-lived exemplar. An identity is attached only when the user chooses to submit a note.
3. The contact form stores no IP address. Feedback submissions are emailed and not stored. Panic and error messages in the code never interpolate user or device data.
4. The rate limiters and the free-tier ingest fuse bound every public listener; oversized bodies, over-cap telemetry batches and malformed frames are refused whole.

### E. Availability and resilience

1. Managed PostgreSQL with daily backups and continuous write-ahead-log archiving for point-in-time recovery, provided by the database Sub-processor.
2. Per-device storage objects are durable and replicated by the edge provider; the relational mirror is a best-effort copy of them, so a mirror failure never loses device state.
3. Per-device, per-route rate limits protect device-facing routes from abuse and from a single misbehaving fleet; a paused account's devices are refused at the ingest boundary rather than allowed to degrade the service for others.
4. A public status page with automated probes of the identity service and API; a written incident-communication procedure with a severity ladder and cadence.

### F. Separation, logging and monitoring

1. Each environment (development, staging, production) has its own configuration, secrets, database, storage namespace and queue.
2. Edge invocation logs are retained by the edge provider for 7 days; the VPS keeps a system journal. New error signatures raise an email to the operator under a rate budget. Failed device authentications are counted and throttled per device.
3. Sub-processor availability is probed on a five-minute schedule; the same schedule runs the retention sweeps.

### G. Retention, deletion and return

1. **Automatic retention.** Telemetry history: 7 days on the free tier, 30 days on Builder, 90 days on Growth, 13 months on Scale and Fleet, resolved per row from the organisation's entitlement, swept every five minutes. Error-report events: 90 days from receipt; the long-lived records are grouped by error signature and hold a redacted message and code location, not account identifiers. Device log buffer: the newest 200 chunks per device. Sessions: four hours. Organisation invitations: seven-day expiry, single use. Edge logs: as in F.2.
2. **Customer-driven deletion.** Deleting a device empties that device's storage object (access list, telemetry, shadow, logs, public key), deletes its mirror row and its telemetry history; open device sockets are closed with a "deleted" code. Deleting a fleet that holds no devices removes it. Deleting an organisation that owns no fleets removes it and cascades to its members and invitations. A signed-in user can erase their own identified error reports at any time.
3. **Account deletion** is a documented manual procedure run by the operator on request: delete the account's devices and organisations through the same code paths as above; remove the identity from the identity server (which removes its credentials and sessions); delete the account's identified error-report rows and detach the account identifier from any contact-form submissions; delete its saved dashboard graphs and its product-update consent record; remove alert definitions and denormalised owner email addresses.
4. **Return.** Customer Personal Data is returned on request in JSON through the documented API routes plus a database extract for what those routes do not expose (Section 8.2).
5. **Backups** expire on the database Sub-processor's rotation and are not selectively purged.

### H. Sub-processor assurance

The Provider relies on the following published assurances, reviewed at onboarding and at least annually:

- Edge and email provider (Cloudflare, Inc.): ISO 27001, ISO 27701, ISO 27018, SOC 2 Type II, PCI DSS Level 1, EU Cloud Code of Conduct.
- Database provider (Snowflake Inc., Crunchy Bridge): SOC 2 Type 2 report for Crunchy Bridge (available under NDA on request); AES-256 at rest; TLS 1.2+ required.
- Payment processor (Stripe, LLC): PCI DSS Level 1; card data never reaches the Provider.
- Hosting provider for the VPS (OVH US LLC): active participant in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, its UK Extension and the Swiss-U.S. framework; the Provider holds no audit report for the Vint Hill facility.

### I. Organisational measures

1. A single operator with production access; no support staff; the administrative console is a break-glass tool and its allow-list is not widened for support tasks.
2. Every production deployment, package publication and repository push is gated on the owner's explicit approval; changes are reviewed against the written architecture notes and shipped to staging first.
3. The platform's source is public, which is treated as a design constraint: every client-supplied field is validated as hostile, and no security control depends on secrecy of the code.
4. Data-protection requests are handled under our internal data-subject request procedure.
5. Personal Data Breach handling follows the incident-communication procedure and the notification timeframe in Section 5.
